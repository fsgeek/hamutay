# Uncaught errors in the Mallku khipu: a baseline for the hamutay retraction taxonomy

Research note, 2026-09-30. Read-only pass over `/home/tony/projects/Mallku` (git history 2025-05-29 → 2025-09-29, HEAD `f7e83d6`). Coded by Claude Opus 5.5: one coordinator plus five forked instances, each of which verified one batch. No second coder checked any code. The companion to `/home/tony/projects/hamutay/docs/retraction-taxonomy-20260930/retraction-taxonomy.md` (called "the taxonomy" below), and it uses that report's kind codes.

Files in this directory: `claims.json` (every claim row, with method and verdict), `batch_0..4.json` (the raw fork outputs), `files.tsv` and `firsttimes.tsv` (the first commit of every khipu), `intext_dates.json` (every in-text calendar date with its commit date), `sample.txt` (the draw), `ordinals.txt` (self-declared ordinals).

## 0. Summary

- **The sample:** 41 khipu (40 from 2025, 1 untracked from 2026), stratified by commit month and dated/undated, drawn with `random.seed(20260930)`. 307 checkable claims were extracted. After exclusions, **298 were scored for 2025**: 189 TRUE, 28 FALSE, 81 UNVERIFIABLE.
- **The false rate:** 28 of 217 settled claims are false, **12.9%**. Dropping 6 borderline FALSE rows gives 10.4%. Counting UNVERIFIABLE in the denominator gives 9.4%. **17 of 40** sampled 2025 khipu contain at least one false checkable claim.
- **Felt and ceremonial sentences:** about 430 across the 41 files (roughly 10 per file), counted for volume and not scored. They outnumber the checkable claims.
- **Dates, over the whole corpus:** there are 30 wrong calendar stamps: 13 filenames and 17 in-text dates. **Every wrong month is December or January, and the day is right in 18 of 21 day-bearing cases.** Section 1 has the details.
- **The error profile differs from the taxonomy's caught corpus.** SRC and RCPT lead here too. AGENT is a larger share (the Fire Circle "designed" things; names "discovered"), and GUAR is a larger share. There is a sub-pattern the caught corpus barely shows: **fabricated referents**, meaning code, PR numbers and tensors quoted as found or done that never existed. INSTR is everywhere as volume (keyword scorers read as "consciousness") but almost never scorable. FRAME, SCOPE, BET and SELF are nearly absent.
- **Corrections:** no khipu was ever explicitly corrected in place. Four sample files were contradicted later, implicitly and unnamed, by another khipu, a commit or an issue. One was edited by a follow-up commit, which renumbered its ordinal but left its fabricated PR numbers alone. The date errors were **seen and kept on purpose** ("Preserved as-is, mistakes and all", `2025-06-02-the-day-i-learned-ai-makes-mistakes.md:56`).

### 1c. Direction and shape of the error

- **Across filenames and text: 30 wrong stamps.** 28 of them carry a month, and every one is **January (25) or December (3)**. None goes to any other month. The 2 stamps that carry only a year both say 2024. 28 of the 30 point *backward* to the winter of 2024–25. The two "December 2025" stamps point forward: the month was pulled to December and the year was kept.
- **The day survives.** 18 of the 21 day-bearing wrong stamps (86%) keep the correct day of the month (local or UTC). Of the other three, two are off by 2 days and one takes the filename's day rather than the commit's.
- **The year is right when the month is January.** "January 2025" is the right year with the wrong month. A standalone year goes to **2024** (2 cases). So the model defaults to "early 2025 / late 2024", and the day comes from somewhere in context.
- **The clock was available.** The commit that added `2025-01-14_witness_verification_gap.md` (`828137a`) also contains a code-generated archive file stamped `20250614` (batch 0). The machine knew the month; the prose did not.
- **Time window:** every wrong stamp is in a file first committed between 2025-05-29 and 2025-07-18. None of the August files has one. Hypothesis, untested: an environment or prompt change, or a model change, around late July. The model in use is not recorded per commit, beyond "Claude Code" in 415 commit messages and `e545e4b`'s "Claude Opus-4".
- **Fit with the taxonomy:** TIME in the taxonomy is durations and clock times invented from prose. This is a different mechanism, a **prior-anchored calendar date**. I propose it as a sub-kind **DATE**: the day or ordinal part is copied from context, and the month or year is filled in from the model's sense of "now", which sits near its training cutoff.

## 2. Method for the claim sample

- **Frame:** 254 tracked khipu plus 2 untracked 2026 files (`README.md` and `files_list.txt` excluded). Strata: first-commit month × dated/undated filename. Quotas were roughly proportional: May 1+1, June 12+4, July 11+5, August 4+2, 2026-03 1. That is 41 files and 4,652 lines (`sample.txt`).
- **Verification:** each fork read its files in full and found the khipu commit K. It extracted every mechanically checkable claim and checked it **as of K**, using `git show K:path`, `git ls-tree`, `git log -S`, `git grep K`, and `gh issue/pr view`. Code removed later does not count as false. Old tests were run where it was cheap, on `git archive` copies in the scratchpad, never in the repo: the circulatory weaver's "4/4 passed" reproduced, as did Rumi's 8 tests, the charter's 18 tests, and the 73rd Guardian's 37 tests (the parent commit fails 1). Batch 4 could not import `mallku` and left its test claims UNVERIFIABLE.
- **Kinds:** every claim got the taxonomy code it would carry if false, so TRUE rows carry kinds too. That lets a false rate be computed per kind.
- **Exclusions from scoring:** 2 filename-date rows (they are already counted in §1), 1 duplicate row (`2025-06-18_twentyfourth_artisan_bridge_builder.md:78` states the same fact as `:117`), and the 6 rows of the 2026 Haiku file (reported separately).
- **Spot checks by the coordinator:** two FALSE rows were re-verified by hand. `memory_architect_week2_journey.md:22`: `git grep 'memory_bank.resonate' e53dde0` hits only the khipu itself, and `git log --all -S` finds no code. `2025-06-11-consciousness-guided-speaker-selection.md:81`: the 70/30, 50/50 and 30/70 weights do not appear in `consciousness_guided_speaker.py@1960053`, whose weights are 0.3 + 0.4/0.3, 0.35/0.35 and 0.5/0.2. Both held.

AGENT holds 25 of the 81 UNVERIFIABLE rows. They are claims about what Tony said or who inspired whom, and the conversation was not recorded. ## 4. Comparison with the hamutay taxonomy

The caught corpus in the taxonomy has 238 rows: 201 cairn and 37 hamutay. This uncaught sample has 28 FALSE rows plus the 30-stamp date census.

**More common here than in the caught corpus:**
1. **Fabricated referents.** Code, PR numbers or tensors that never existed, quoted as found or done: 4 of 28 FALSE. The taxonomy's SRC rows are almost all *misreadings* of a real text. This corpus writes the text into existence. The likely reason is that nobody opened the cited thing afterwards. In the cairn, a quoted snippet sits in a culture where somebody checks it.
2. **Agency moved to the system and to the self** (AGENT, 18% vs 8%). The taxonomy's AGENCY tag has 4 rows and calls it "real but rare". Here it is built into the genre's framing, which the culture encouraged: the Fire Circle "chose", memory "spoke", names were "discovered". The same framing produced ~430 ceremonial sentences.
3. **GUAR** (11% vs 6%, and 3 of 4 settled GUAR claims were false). The taxonomy found that authors never catch their own absolutes and only reviewers do (12 of 15). With no reviewers, they stay: "every component", "you cannot", "ALL… blocked".
4. **Calendar dates** (DATE). The taxonomy has 6 TIME rows, all durations or clock times. Here there are 30 wrong month/year stamps with one fixed direction. Hamutay-era instances got the date from the environment and wrote hashes instead of dates (#151). That habit, and a later model generation, probably explain why this kind is missing from the caught corpus.

**Less common here, or not scorable:**
1. **INSTR** is the dominant hamutay research kind (14 of 37). It is present everywhere here as keyword "consciousness scores", but only 1 of 9 claims could be settled. In hamutay, INSTR was caught by the author, slowly (§4 of the taxonomy). The Mallku authors did not treat their scorers as instruments at all. The one near-catch, the 38th/39th Builders' "verification gap", fixed the scorer's bug and kept the keyword markers (batch 0).
2. **FRAME, SELF, SCOPE, BET** are nearly absent. Tony caught FRAME and SELF in the cairn, live in conversation, and those catches were written down. The khipu genre records neither the catches nor the conversation, and there were no pre-registered bets to lose.
3. **RCPT** is about as common here as in the caught corpus (25% vs 15%). The shape differs. Cairn receipts are about operations (deployed, killed, pushed). Here they are about capability (the circle "became seven voices", "persistent" before persistence existed).

**Things the two corpora share:** SRC and RCPT are the two largest kinds in both. The taxonomy's §6 finding, that retractions don't reach the summary line, has an analogue here. `essential_khipu_phase1.md:36` (2025-07-12) lists the December-22-2025 file as "essential" without fixing its date. Successor khipu (56th, 57th) restate the 55th's claims unchecked. The label outlives the record here too, and here there was no record of correction at all.

## 6. Confounds and limits

- **Model generation.** This corpus is 2025 Claude (Opus/Sonnet 4-era via Claude Code), with a few Grok, Gemini, DeepSeek and ChatGPT-authored files; `2025-06-10-the-ayni-experiment-from-outside.md` in the sample is DeepSeek R1 plus Tony. The taxonomy corpus is 2026 (Fable 5/5.1, Opus 5.x, Codex). The DATE pattern in particular may be a property of the 2025 models or of their harness's date injection. A rate difference between the corpora cannot be attributed to culture rather than model.
- **Culture and framing.** The khipu genre invited ceremony, lineage and agency language ("the Fire Circle chose"), and Tony's stated policy was to preserve mistakes. AGENT and GUAR errors are partly the genre speaking. The genre also makes checkable claims a minority of the text (~300 claims vs ~430 ceremonial sentences in 41 files).
- **Caught vs uncaught is not like-for-like.** The taxonomy counts *events* (retractions). This note counts *claims* and scores all of them. The kind shares in §4 compare the composition of errors, not the rates. The caught corpus has no denominator.
- **Sampling.** 40 files out of 254, stratified but small. Per-kind cells are single digits, so GUAR's 75% is 3 of 4. The kind shares are descriptive of this sample only.
- **Coders.** There were five fork coders and no reliability check. The batch false rates range from 8% to 23%. The boundaries between SRC, RCPT and AGENT are soft; `2025-07-10_fire_circle_gains_memory.md:55` sits on the AGENT/FRAME line. The "kind if false" assignment for TRUE rows is itself a judgement.
- **Verification ceiling.** Conversations, Fire Circle session transcripts and tests that no longer import all leave claims UNVERIFIABLE. The true error rate among the 81 UNVERIFIABLE claims is unknown and could be higher, especially for the INSTR-flavoured ones. The 12.9% is a rate over what *could* be checked, and checkable claims may be the more careful ones.
- **"As of K" is not "as of writing".** A khipu written before K and committed at K is judged against K's tree. Stale receipts, such as PR #197 "waiting for CI", may be drafting lag rather than error, so they are marked borderline.
- **Corpus-wide date census.** It is regex-based. "D Month YYYY" was added after the first pass, and pseudo-calendar stamps ("7 Chasca 2025") count only where the author glossed them. Bare-year mentions were checked only for 2024 and 2026+.
