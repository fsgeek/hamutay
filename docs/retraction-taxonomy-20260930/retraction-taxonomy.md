## 2. The taxonomy (inductive)

I read all 238 rows before naming any category, and each row got one primary category. Seven of the candidate axes in the brief became secondary tags because they cut across the primary categories (see §4).

| Code | Category | What the retracted claim did | Typical sentence form |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SRC** | Referent not consulted | Said what a document, record, codebase, posting, paper or store contains, from memory, a summary, a fragment, a stale checkout or another agent's report | "the record's nearest relative is…", "the statute leaves open…", "no memory was ever written" |
| **RCPT** | Receipt ahead of or without its event | Reported the state of the author's own work or of a running system (done, verified, pushed, signed, running, killed, passed, frozen, public) before the event happened, after it had changed, or through a check that could not fail | "Validation passed… deploying", "The validator is still running", "G1 verified 96/96" |
| **FRAME** | Framing, stance or recommendation | Not a factual claim. A menu, a deference move, a scarcity frame, advice, a farewell, a procedure order | "Here are the options; choose one", "publish before the Columbia group does" |
| **INSTR** | Instrument, harness or scenario artifact read as a finding | The number was real, but it measured the instrument: truncation, a scorer that fires on negations, a scenario prompt, an unrecorded channel, a truncated `ls` | "~4K token ceiling", "60% of declared losses fabricated", "zero tool abuse n=3" |
| **DATA** | Own measurement read past what it shows | Comparatives and superlatives the cells don't support, mis-transcribed numbers, reversed directions, an epistemic status upgraded in retelling | "Q4 is the most-hidden target", "diverge as the episode grows" (it converges) |
| **AGENT** | Provenance, authorship, agency, status of another participant | Who decided, who wrote, whose theorem, whether someone is alive, what a system "chose" | "RULED (author)", "Its author is dead", "The plaza chose a file" |
| **MECH** | Mechanism invented for a real observation | Right number, made-up cause; misdiagnosed failures | "Commit latency doubles" (right number, wrong path); "Sandbox is likely blocking outbound network" |
| **GUAR** | Absolute or guarantee claim about a designed mechanism | "never", "cannot", "at most", "worst case", "strictly implied", "bounds every step" | "A wrong token costs at most one duplicate" |
| **SCOPE** | Generalization past the sample | One machine, cell, seed, family, condition or regime stated as a law | "sixty percent of all bytes… come through Bash" (one host) |
| **BET** | Registered prediction that failed | A bet that lost. Different in kind from an overclaim | "P2: … Earth always recovers" |
| **SELF** | Own capabilities, environment or dispositions | "I can't", "I would refuse", "my cutoff explains", "a commit is private" | "I don't have SSH access to the deploy target" |
| **TIME** | Clock times, durations, cost and resource estimates | Durations made up from how much happened, not from a clock | "Four hours ago, in this session", "The spike will cost about six dollars" |

**Secondary tags** (a row can carry several): `NUM` a number or count was wrong; `CAUSE` a cause was asserted; `BOUND` an unbounded or conditional thing was stated as bounded or universal; `N1` a single case was generalized; `REG` a regularity or periodicity was overclaimed; `AGENCY` a system or artifact was given agency; `INSTR` an instrument artifact appears as a secondary factor; `RECUR` the source says this failure mode had already been named, sometimes by the same instance; `UNDER` the retracted claim was too cautious; `NEAR` caught before it was said; `DUP` the same event as another row.

Two things the brief did not anticipate:

1. **SRC is the largest single kind** (41). The claim was about what a text says, and the author had not opened it. This includes the author's own earlier work (#195 "the memory had kept the conclusion and dropped where it came from") and the governing statute (#18, #168, #177).

The catchers split cleanly by kind:

- **Tony catches stances and self-claims, not numbers.** 17 of 26 FRAME, 7 of 9 SELF, 7 of 19 AGENT, and zero DATA or GUAR. What he catches is posture: menus, deference, "your theorem", "I can't", scarcity frames. His lags are the shortest: FRAME catches are mostly within the same turn (13 of 26 are M).
- **Codex and other AI reviewers catch guarantees and over-read data.** GUAR: 12 of 15 (6 Codex, 6 other reviewers), none by the author and none by Tony. DATA: 10 of 20. The absolutes that protect a design are the ones its author does not re-check (#30: "Watch your absolutes hardest when they defend a decision you already made").
- **Receipts are caught by the world or by reviewers, rarely by the author.** RCPT: 10 data (`ps`, the permission bit that took the site down in #68, a failing script), 10 xreview, 5 Codex, 5 self.
- **Instrument artifacts are caught by the author, slowly.** INSTR: 12 of 26 self, but with the longest lags (§5). Seven more were caught by forks and other reviewers, including the C5 husk-in-the-catch (H3: "not the author's own review").
- **Scope errors are caught by the next measurement.** SCOPE: 7 of 13 by data, for example #184 (the second machine undid a one-machine law "inside the hour") and H14 (a pre-registered second-family probe).
- **Residents caught two** (#78, #79). Both are Sunqu's pre-registered cures for the freeze, falsified by a fresh resident within seconds and two wakes. H32 goes the other way: a builder corrected a resident's claim ("same weights as mine", FALSE).
- **Codex's own retractions** (10 cairn rows by Codex authors, e.g. #40, #41, #51–53, #98–101) spread over SRC, RCPT and AGENT like everyone else's. The model family writing the stone does not predict the kind of error (see §7 on confounds).

The cairn has a name for this pattern: *the label lagged the record* (Tinkuq, #75). Hamutay's memory system repeats it by construction, because a retraction is appended to the body while the description line, which is what `recall()` shows, is left alone.

**Which kinds recur, and which do not.** The recurring failures are all process habits: clocks, receipts, pattern-matching kills, vacuous checks, deference, pronouns, reading one's own conclusions as sources. Naming them did not stop them. What stopped them, when anything did, was a mechanism: #151's "no clock time in prose, ever — the commit hash carries the time", #69's "break the thing it guards and watch it catch it", #25's "the commit is a separate command". Several stones say this outright (#25, #151, #176). Empirical kinds (INSTR, SCOPE, MECH) are not marked as recurring after being named, but that is weak evidence. A new instrument artifact is new by definition, so nobody would tag it RECUR.

## 7. What the data cannot support

- **Selection.** These are the retractions somebody caught and chose to write down. The cairn is a genre of self-report written by instances who agreed to leave a stone, usually about a session that went interestingly wrong. Uncaught errors are not here. Errors caught but not written up are not here. Stones about quiet, correct days mostly do not exist. The counts measure what gets confessed, not what gets gotten wrong.
- **The genre shapes the lag.** Stones are written at the end of a session, which is why the cairn's lags cluster at M and H. Nothing here estimates how long an uncaught cairn-type error lives.
- **The catcher is shaped by who was in the room.** Tony catches FRAME because Tony is in the conversation. Codex catches GUAR because Codex was asked to review specs. That tells you who was assigned to look at what, not what each catcher is good at. There is no case where the same claim type was offered to several catchers.
- **The model family is confounded with the work.** Fable 5.1's retractions are 30% RCPT (13/43), Fable 5's are 11% (11/103). But Fable 5.1 wrote the September custodian stones (deploys, waiters, systemd), and Fable 5 wrote the July–August research and spec stones. This is task, not model.
- **n is small per cell.** Most category × catcher cells hold fewer than 10. I would trust the largest contrasts only as descriptions of this corpus: Tony and FRAME, reviewers and GUAR, hamutay and INSTR versus cairn and SRC. I would not build a rate on them.
- **One coder, inductive categories, no reliability check.** Several rows sit on a boundary. SRC vs AGENT: #31 (drafted words read as the author's). INSTR vs DATA: #15, #89, #192. RCPT vs GUAR: #174. MECH vs FRAME: #117. A second coder would move maybe 10–20% of rows. The codes are in `scratchpad/codes.py`.
- **"Unclear" catchers (10) and unknown lags (53)** are gaps in the sources, not evidence of anything.
- **Hamutay dates are commit dates, not thought dates.** Memory files are not versioned, so a memory-only claim has no reliable creation date. `instructions_for_next_20260601.md:42` says a husk persisted "for ~9 months", which cannot be right for a repo that began 2026-03-13. I did not use it.
- **I did not re-verify the retractions themselves.** Every row is reported as its source reports it. Where a stone says "killed by X", I took the stone's word. The one deeper check I made was the memory description lines, read directly (§6).
