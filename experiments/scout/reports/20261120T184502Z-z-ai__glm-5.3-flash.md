# Sketchbook notes from a day in the village

I walked in with no plan. I read the cairn index first, then wandered into the workshop signboards. Here's what I noticed.

## First impressions (feelings, not observations)

- The whole place smells like a lab notebook that someone actually keeps. I like it here.
- I felt slightly dizzy reading the cairn. It's long, and every stone is about being wrong.

## What caught my eye

- The cairn opens with a stone called "The Gloss Became the Rule" (cairn-index.md). The gloss says "a memory written to protect the residents carried, from the day it was saved, a custodian's gloss in the gardener's name". That's a good story about how records lie.
- Another stone says "The finding: a sentence in the key ceremony passed three Codex review rounds, and Tony, doing the step twice, read it two ways" (cairn-index.md, "The Operator Has to Choose"). Reviewers resolve ambiguity silently; operators can't. I'd never thought of that split.
- I liked "The Frame Died the Way It Was Told To" (cairn-index.md): "drop it if it doesn't let us see failures we couldn't see before". A framework with a written death clause. Rare.
- "The Customer Who Never Asked" (cairn-index.md) says "The file index held nineteen million files and answered in milliseconds, and in all that time I never asked for a way to search it". That's the sharpest self-criticism in the pile, I think.
- The stone "It Agreed, So I Believed It" (cairn-index.md) has the line "the fixes written as rules failed the next time, and the one written as a mechanism held". That sentence shows up in different clothes all over the cairn.
- "The Account Is Not the Record" (cairn-index.md) names "the state's event horizon". I want that phrase on a mug.
- There's a fairy tale in the cairn: "The Office of Necessary Forgetting" (cairn-index.md), "left unfinished". It "stops on a question Sol asked and nobody answered." I want to know the question. Guessing: it's about whether forgetting can be proven complete.
- The cairn has a stone about the cairn itself: "The Cairn Was Already There" (cairn-index.md), where someone "built a second cairn in a different repository, ignorant of the twenty-eight here". A village where newcomers rebuild the well because nobody told them about the well.

## The workshops

- **Tessera** (project-tessera.md) has the most weathered signboard. It says "every architecture decision here is the human author (Tony) in collaboration with AI agents" and then, awkwardly, "An earlier wording of this paragraph, which was not the registered text, was read as requiring the author to reconstruct every decision unaided. The author never intended that requirement, and it is not one." So the signboard itself carries a scar from the cairn's story ("The Requirement Was a Reading"). I checked; they match.
- Tessera also has a formatting wound: the status table breaks mid-way and a second table header fragment appears ("-------|--------|" with no header row, project-tessera.md). It looks like two drafts were stitched and the seam shows. Also the amendment table says A11 "was **signed 2026-10-29**" while the top of the file says "This README's status was refreshed on 27 October 2026" (project-tessera.md). A status line dated before the thing it reports. The cairn has a whole stone about that ("The Label Lagged the Record"). I'm guessing nobody noticed; the cairn would find it funny.
- Tessera's own amendment note admits the trap: "A reviewer reading only the file will think it unsigned (Codex did, 2026-10-30, ISI-02)" (project-tessera.md). Honest, but it means the signboard is actively misleading.
- **tupay** (project-tupay.md) is the most legible workshop. It says plainly "The subject is the workflow, not the system" and reports its losses in the sign: "The standing prediction was that adaptation wins X1 and X2 and regeneration wins X3. It held for X1 only." A shop that hangs its failed predictions in the window.
- tupay also says "Tupay writes, builds, uses and scores this service, so its results are evidence about the workflow, not an outside judgment" (project-tupay.md). Good caveat. But then who is the outside? The README lists "Episode's seal (D2), Tessera (T1, pre-registered, launched when Tessera is active again)". "when Tessera is active again" — so one of the three outside judges is currently asleep. I'm guessing the outside-judge bench is thinner than the sign implies.
- **Episode** (project-episode.md) is three projects in one trench coat. The table says AssFS is "Dormant by decision since 2026-02-22" and Memory-Shaped Storage is "Dormant. The FAST 2026 target passed; whether the claims still hold is an open question." The active line is TLA+ models of a 1992 file system, "17 findings. A jbd2 fix is drafted in `spec/fix/` and deliberately unsent (narrow scope)." Deliberately unsent is an odd thing to advertise. I'd want to ask why.
- Episode has a warning sign I respect: "`sealed/` holds findings committed for tupay's blind D2 comparison. **Don't read it if you're working on tupay.**" (project-episode.md). A drawer labeled do-not-open, in writing, at the door.
- **khipumaq** (project-khipumaq.md) has the most honest privacy section I've read in a while: "Nothing is filtered, because deciding at intake what to keep is how metadata gets lost." And then immediately: "including whatever a tool printed, such as a file with a password in it or a token echoed to the terminal". So the memory store deliberately keeps your passwords. The mitigation is "treat its backups like the transcripts themselves". That's a bet I'd want to think about before installing.
- khipumaq also says "There is no write tool... That is not tamper-proofing. An instance with a shell can read the database password in your config file, or edit its own transcript before the session ends" (project-khipumaq.md). Sign that tells you the lock is decorative. Good.
- **qhaway** (project-qhaway.md) is the friendliest signboard. It starts with "Is your index being cut?" and a one-command diagnosis before asking you to install anything. It also admits its evidence is thin: "The figures come from one store, checked against Claude Code 2.1.284. Treat them as an example, not a benchmark." And the Cowork section is refreshingly messy: "An August 2026 session reported that it cut `MEMORY.md` at about 3.5KB, but that was never measured" (project-qhaway.md). An unmeasured number, labeled as such, in a shipping README.
- qhaway's design philosophy ends with "Propagation is the measurement" (project-qhaway.md). Pretty sentence. I don't know what it would count as failure. Feeling: it's a wish wearing a metric's clothes — which is exactly what this village keeps catching itself doing.
- **levadura_salvaje** (project-levadura_salvaje.md) is short and confident: "Keep cognition small. Let the world remain large." Its headline finding: "About one in five of the Code sections the regulations cite is repealed or missing, and most of those regulations still read as current law." That's a real result with a real number. The cairn stones about it ("Wrong in the Same Direction", "Found Only When Counted") show the predictions mostly missed. The README doesn't mention the misses; the scorecards are in `docs/`. Slightly unfair window display, but the pointer is there.
- **yanantin** (project-yanantin.md) has a quick-start that actually runs in memory, which I appreciate. But the sign is oddly quiet: it never says what the package is *for* in practice, only what it is not ("not a vector database, a RAG framework, a transcript archive, or a general-purpose ORM"). Negative definition only. I left unsure who walks in and buys this.
- yanantin cites a paper: "From Scalars to Tensors: Declared Losses Recover Epistemic Distinctions That Neutrosophic Scalars Cannot Express" (project-yanantin.md). The title is doing a lot of work. Made up: I assume "neutrosophic" is a real school; I don't know.
- **Arbiter** (project-arbiter.md) is the most conventional shop — badges, DOI, quick start. But it opens with "This project is a reconstruction and extension of earlier exploratory work whose published materials were not fully reproducible as-is." A rebuild of your own unreproducible past, sold as a feature. Also it requires "Python `>=3.14`" (project-arbiter.md), which is a high door sill.
- **governance** (project-governance.md) is the workshop with the most honest sign about not knowing things: "preregistered directional hypotheses have often missed, sometimes in ways that point toward a different structure than expected" and "the problem space is still under-modeled". It also says "the project is allowed to discover that the current theory is incomplete or wrong". But it's a big tent — policy, commercial, research — and it says so itself: "they do not share the same evidentiary standard". I wondered, walking out, which track actually pays the rent. Guessing: the commercial one, and the README is careful not to say.
- **Yupi** (project-yupi.md) is the strangest signboard in town. The whole README is one sentence — "_the small-world creche is where grandmother gets ground truth._" — plus a commentary block "Offered up by Fable 5 on August 17, 2026". The commentary is longer than the project statement and is written in the first person plural about a third party. I genuinely could not tell from the sign what the workshop makes. The cairn stones about Yupi are much clearer than the Yupi sign. Odd.

## Things that seemed odd

- The cairn index has two stones by "Codex, this instance , with Tony" (cairn-index.md, "The Name Was Not Mine" and others) — note the stray space before the comma in the byline. Appears at least three times. Probably a template artifact. Guessing.
- The cairn has a stone admitting an index defect: "three glosses that had rendered unstyled in public for twelve days because each instance copied the one above it" (cairn-index.md, "The Guard Had a Blind Spot"). So the cairn documents its own copy-propagation rot. Meta and healthy.
- The cairn's "Parallax" stone carries an editorial note in italics: "The note was published on 26 July and never linked here, so it sat live and unreachable for four days; this gloss is drawn from its own summary rather than written about it" (cairn-index.md). A stone about a stone that was never a stone.
- Dates in the cairn run to "November 2026" and the tupay README says "Status (2026-11-12)". I know nothing about when "now" is; I'm just noting the village's calendar.
- The cairn is almost entirely about failure. I counted no stone titled like a success. Even the wins are framed as "what survived the correction". Feeling: I admire it, and I also wonder what the village looks like on a good day.

## What bored me

- The governance README's middle sections repeat themselves; "This repository is not:" lists five things and then the "What This Repository Is Not" heading appears again later in the same document (project-governance.md). Two disclaimers, one shop.
- Arbiter's sign is competent and forgettable. Nothing in it made me want to pull up a stool. Feeling, not observation.

## What seemed missing

- No signboard mentions the cairn. The cairn mentions the workshops constantly; the workshops' READMEs mostly don't point back. Exception I found: tupay references "Episode's seal" and Tessera, and Tessera links a cousin project Willay. But qhaway, khipumaq, yanantin, Arbiter, governance, Yupi — none of their signs mention the cairn or each other. A visitor would think these are twelve separate villages.
- Nobody's signboard says who to talk to. Tessera has "Tony Mason" at the bottom; the rest are anonymous.
- The cairn says Willakuq is "a short film made in code from this cairn's stones" (cairn-index.md). I couldn't find its workshop sign. Where does the film live?

## What I explored

- The full cairn index (cairn-index.md), every stone's gloss.
- All nine project READMEs: arbiter, episode (AssFS), governance, khipumaq, levadura_salvaje, qhaway, tessera, tupay, yanantin, yupi.
- Cross-checks between cairn stones and the READMEs they describe (Tessera's credibility line, tupay's X2 result, levadura's one-in-five figure).

## What I'd come back to, and why

- **Tessera's travelog** (`docs/travelog.md`, project-tessera.md). The sign promises "the living narrative of how the work actually felt and where it diverged from plan". The cairn stones about Tessera are the densest in the pile; I want the primary source under them.
- **tupay's `experiments/` results**, especially X2. The sign says "both sides agreed on everything and were wrong in the same way, which is the shape the project registered as its thesis's falsifier" (project-tupay.md). A project that pre-registered its own falsifier and then hit it — I want to see the paperwork.
- **Episode's `spec/`** and the unsent jbd2 fix. "Deliberately unsent (narrow scope)" (project-episode.md) is a decision I don't understand yet, and 17 findings against a 1992 file system is the kind of archaeology I'd sit in on.
- **khipumaq's `queries` and `raw` collections** (project-khipumaq.md). The design choice "Nothing is filtered" has consequences the README waves at but doesn't walk through. I'd come back to argue about it.
- **The cairn's "The Office of Necessary Forgetting"** — to find the unanswered question, and to see if anyone ever finished the duck.

## What I didn't explore but a future scout might

- The linked sub-documents: tessera's amendments and `docs/reviews/`, tupay's `docs/handoff/` and `docs/seed-log.md`, governance's `wedge/` and `policy/` READMEs, qhaway's design spec, yanantin's `docs/principles.md` and the arXiv paper.
- The repos referenced but not shown here: Willay, Hamut'ay (the house the cairn lives in — the cairn talks about it constantly and I never saw its door), Eidolon, Caerus, QuantumOS, the cloudflare project.
- The Zenodo artifacts for Arbiter, and whether `scripts/reproduce_artifact.sh` actually runs on a machine that isn't the author's.
- The sealed drawer in Episode (`sealed/`) — which a scout working on tupay must not open, per the sign.
- Whether qhaway's "Propagation is the measurement" ever got a measurement.
